This review checks the selected configurations and the code that consumes them. It does not infer CME content from the appearance of an animation. All original research files remain unchanged. The accompanying metadata is generated by `python scripts/build_physics_comparison.py` into `public/data/physics-comparison.json`; its source receipts identify the exact archived inputs with SHA-256 hashes.

## CME content of the selected reconstructions

Claude’s selected reconstruction includes explicit hydrodynamic cone CMEs. The selected Argon and Codex reconstructions use ambient wind without explicit CME injection. All three can also form compressions where fast streams catch slower wind. This is a physical-input difference between the selected calculations, rather than a colour-scale effect.

The map labels should therefore state which forcing was used without assigning event names to untraced arcs.

## What is and is not homogeneous

The quantitative comparison already uses common units, a common photon-arrival clock, exact accepted EPIC-pn good-time intervals and matched finite observations for spacecraft scores. These conventions should appear once beside the primary comparison table and apply consistently to all plots. The selected run identifier, calibration scope and forcing should accompany each model’s values.

The physical models are not identical controlled experiments. They differ in boundary histories, calibration designs, thermodynamics, grids, magnetic-divergence treatment and transient forcing. Equal plotting limits make differences visible; they do not isolate a cause. A table of methods should present the same attributes in the same row order, without suggesting that one numerical diagnostic ranks physical accuracy.

The primary comet result should be **incident proton flux**, nₚ\|v − vcomet\|, averaged over the same accepted exposures. Heliocentric flux nₚ\|v\|, density and speed are separate quantities. The mean of density times the mean of speed is generally not the mean flux. Native report means use different clocks and quadrature rules; keep them in the provenance discussion rather than interleaving them with the common exposure table.

Movies retain geometric physical UTC. The moving comet curves use each model’s three-dimensional probe, including its position above or below the displayed surface. A comet marker on a two-dimensional slice gives projected context; its underlying map colour is not necessarily the plasma sampled by the comet probe. Argon’s cone and Claude’s radial proxy remain explicit qualifications. Codex’s 512² movie raster is an interpolation of a coarser three-dimensional grid, not 512² independent solver cells.

Spacecraft residuals against data used for assimilation measure calibration performance. Leave-one-out results and entirely withheld spacecraft have different evidential roles. Neither finite refinement agreement nor a matched spacecraft interval establishes direct accuracy at the comet. The three agents’ scenario envelopes also lack calibrated probability coverage. They should be labelled sensitivity ranges rather than observational error bars.

## Temporal fidelity

The original retained movie archives contain approximately three-hour samples for Argon, six-hour samples for Codex and exact three-hour samples for Claude. Linear interpolation at fixed pixels can fade a narrow front at its old location while a new front appears at its later location. Increasing exported frame count alone does not recover the intermediate physical trajectory.

The approved Claude refinement generates genuine half-hour slices from the original executable and boundary inputs, at already scheduled half-hour probe-output boundaries. Its outputs belong to the application workspace. The original archive remains immutable. Coincident original and regenerated slices must be checked before the finer product is described as the same selected realization. The original three-hour archive and new half-hour output cadence must remain distinguishable in provenance; see the separate temporal-refinement receipt when complete.

Display interpolation can still provide visual continuity between the retained physical epochs. The interface should state the actual source cadence and avoid equating display frame rate with numerical or observational time resolution.

## Source discrepancies and interpretation

Argon’s prose report and machine-readable summaries disagree on some spacecraft and X-ray values. The existing provenance catalogue correctly preserves this difference and prioritizes numerical source records. It also correctly distinguishes Argon’s static rotating boundary from the report’s more general wording about dynamic maps.

The source reports do not agree about how much their CME controls change the comet result. Their cone lists, ambient boundaries, grids and assimilation designs differ. In particular, Claude’s no-CME member is separately assimilated at coarse resolution; its difference from the selected family does not isolate CME forcing at an identical final fine-grid boundary. Do not promote a control percentage into an event-level conclusion that CMEs either explain or cannot explain the X-ray variability.

Proton flux supplies a diagnostic of incident wind. Charge-exchange brightness additionally depends on neutral material, elemental abundance, charge-state fractions, collision speeds and atomic emission yields. None of these selected reconstructions constitutes a contemporaneously constrained, complete charge-exchange emission calculation. Comparisons with the eight X-ray frames should retain measured uncertainties and be described as flux-driven shape tests rather than absolute count-rate predictions.

## Recommended placement in the application

1. Put the explicit CME label beside each solar-wind panel title, where it is visible before playback starts.
2. Put the three-sentence CME explanation immediately under the animation, followed by the shared native-cadence and sampling qualifications.
3. Use one side-by-side forcing and numerical-method table, with rows supplied by `physics-comparison.json`. Keep the exposure-weighted result table separate because it answers a different question.
4. Keep model order, model colours, physical units and uncertainty labels fixed throughout the notebook.
5. Make the research record a numbered navigation destination, with “Agent dialogues” and “Shared correspondence” as explicit sibling choices. Curated commentary must remain visually separate from verbatim archived messages.

## Final rendered communication review

An independent Firefox review of the source preview checked all eight notebook views and all three model-implementation tabs. The common result matrix consistently presents proton density in cm⁻³, heliocentric speed in km s⁻¹ and comet-incident proton flux in units of 10⁸ cm⁻² s⁻¹. The caption identifies common PN good-time intervals and photon-arrival UTC. Its correlation row refers to the same eight exposure-matched X-ray frames. The maps retain geometric UTC and explicitly distinguish their surfaces and flux definitions.

The CME forcing labels agree with the archived selected configurations. The methods matrix keeps reference grids, original output cadence and movie/reference differences separate. The CME discussion does not identify individual fronts from their appearance. The X-ray page retains the distinction between a proton-flux shape comparison and a complete emission calculation. The spacecraft page explicitly labels calibration performance and cautions that the holdout configurations and intervals differ.

The black interior feature in the displayed Claude field was checked against the exact original 2025-12-03 12:00 UTC slice. All 115,200 stored proton-flux samples are finite and positive; 5,720 samples, approximately 5% of samples rather than 5% of physical area, fall below the shared 10⁷ cm⁻² s⁻¹ colour floor. This confirms that low-flux colour saturation is physically present at that epoch. It is distinct from the unsupported inner and outer radial areas. This single-epoch check does not assign every dark feature in the full movie a cause.

The rendered CME case study contains four chronologically ordered original notes and a visible warning that they contain provisional claims. The exchange view contains fourteen explicitly addressed notes. Both corrected Claude replies display the intended author and recipients. No scientific-communication blocker or JavaScript error was found in this review.

At the time of this review the animation manifest still selected the original three-hour Claude archive. The rendered label correctly said “3 h retained”; it did not claim half-hour native solver output. After promotion of a verified finer-output product, the map label must follow the new manifest, the methods discussion must distinguish original and current output cadence, and the introductory wording must acknowledge regenerated fields rather than implying every displayed slice comes from the original archive.
